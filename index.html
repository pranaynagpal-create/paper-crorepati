    The rules behind the numbers

    At exercise

    • The perquisite is (fair market value on the exercise date minus your exercise price) × shares. It is taxed as salary at your slab, with 4% cess, and surcharge when your income for the year crosses ₹50 L (10%), ₹1 Cr (15%) or ₹2 Cr (25%, the new-regime cap). "Decide from the gain size" applies the surcharge band that the ESOP gain alone would put you in.
    • For unlisted shares the FMV comes from a SEBI Category-I merchant banker report not older than 180 days. For listed shares it is the market price.
    • If you exercise at exit and sell the same day, the whole gain is perquisite. If you exercise early, only the gain up to that day's FMV is perquisite, and the rest is capital gain later. That is why "exercise now" can net more, at the cost of real cash today and the risk that the exit never comes.

    Startup deferral

    • Only an employee of a DPIIT-recognised startup that also holds an Inter-Ministerial Board certificate under Section 140 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (Section 80-IAC of the 1961 Act) can defer the perquisite tax. The amount does not change, only the date. It becomes payable at the earliest of: you sell the shares, you leave the company, or 60 months pass from the end of the tax year of allotment (48 months under the old Act).
    • About 3,700 of roughly 1.97 lakh DPIIT startups held that certificate as of April 2025, so the default here is "no".
    • Leaving the company ends the deferral, so the month-18 card never uses it.

    At sale

    • Your cost of acquisition is the FMV used for the perquisite, so you are not taxed twice on the same rupee.
    • Unlisted shares held more than 24 months: long-term, 12.5% without indexation, no ₹1.25 L exemption. Held 24 months or less: short-term, at your slab.
    • Listed shares held more than 12 months: 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 L. Held 12 months or less: 20%. Cess of 4% is added to both. Surcharge on capital gains is ignored here.

    Exit price and vesting

    • Exit price per share = today's FMV × growth multiple × (1 minus dilution). Dilution is the share of the company sold to future investors before the exit.
    • Vesting follows your schedule. With a 12-month cliff and annual vesting, month 18 means one year's tranche. Monthly vesting after the cliff would give 37.5% at month 18 on a 4-year plan.
    • When you leave, most plans give 30 to 90 days to exercise what has vested. You pay the exercise price and the perquisite tax then, in cash, with no sale to fund it. Unexercised options lapse. Unvested options are gone the day you leave.
